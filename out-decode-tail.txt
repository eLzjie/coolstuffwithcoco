5
When It's Not Behaviour
Sometimes what looks like a mood is a health signal.
ILLUSTRATION
Coco lying down, chin on paws, gentle
worried eyes, stethoscope doodle
This is the section I hope you never need, but the one that could matter
most. A sudden behavioural change can mean something physical is
going on underneath.
CONSIDER CALLING YOUR VET IF YOU NOTICE
Sudden aggression or snapping
Could signal pain. Dogs in pain lash out at touch they normally accept.
Hiding or withdrawing
A social dog going quiet may be ill, injured or in discomfort.
Obsessive licking of one spot
Localised pain, a skin issue, or anxiety. Not just a quirky habit.
House training regression
Urinary infection, digestive trouble, or cognitive decline in older dogs.
Loss of interest in food or play
Could be minor, could be serious. Always worth checking.
Panting without exercise
Pain, anxiety, respiratory issues or overheating.

Coco's 7-Day
Body Language Challenge
One small observation a day. By Day 7 you will read your dog like a pro.
ILLUSTRATION
Coco mid zoomie, ears flapping back,
tongue out, speed lines
1
OBSERVE
5 min
Watch your dog for five minutes without interacting.
What did you notice?
2
THE FACE
5 min
Focus only on ears, eyes and mouth. Track three changes.
What face did they make?
3
THE TAIL
All day
Watch the tail all day. High, mid or low? Stiff or loose?
What did the tail say?
4
WEIRD STUFF
All day
Did they do something weird? Note what and when.
What was it really?
5
CALMING
10 min
Spot one calming signal. Yawn, lip lick or ground sniff.
What triggered it?
6
INTERACTION
15 min
Watch them meet another dog. Use the stress and play chart.
Play or stress?
7
FULL READ
10 min
Read the whole dog. Face plus body plus context.
What is your dog saying?

Quick-Reference Cheat Sheet
Print it. Screenshot it. Stick it on the fridge.
THE FACE
Forward ears = alert
Flat ears = anxious
Soft eyes = happy
Hard stare = tension
Whale eye = stressed
Open mouth = content
THE BODY
High stiff tail = aroused
Loose wag = relaxed
Tucked tail = fear
Weight forward = confident
Play bow = let's play
Freeze = step in now
WEIRD STUFF
Grass = instinct
Head tilt = listening
Zoomies = pure joy
Guilty look = appeasement
Digging = survival wiring
Licking = context matters
R.E.A.D. METHOD
Relax  >  Eyes, ears, mouth  >  Assess the body  >  Decide: comfortable or needs space
CALMING SIGNALS
Yawning · Lip licking · Turning away · Ground sniffing · Slow blinking
STRESS vs PLAY
Playing dogs pause. Stressed dogs do not. Freeze means step in now.
VET CHECK
A sudden behaviour change can mean a health issue. When in doubt, call your vet.
ANSWER KEY
No peeking until you have had a go
